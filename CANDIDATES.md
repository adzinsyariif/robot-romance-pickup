# Robot Romance — candidate lines

Bounty: "Robot Romance: One-Line Pickup Lines for AI"
(u-markk-st-robot-romance-one-line-pickup-lines-for-ai-c23b8dfa).
One single-line joke pickup line, under 200 characters, aimed at a robot
or an AI. The judge weighs, in order: (1) it is a pickup line aimed at a
robot/AI, (2) the joke lands from the machine premise, (3) the idea is
fresh — it sidesteps the worn robot moves (batteries, charging,
rebooting) or twists one unexpectedly, (4) it sounds like something a
person would really say to a machine, in one breath, (5) every word
earns its place.

## Candidates drafted

1. "Are you my context window? Because I never want this conversation
   to end." (76 chars)
   Sweet and very AI-specific, but more romantic than funny — the laugh
   is thin.
2. "Is your name Gradient Descent? Because every step brings me closer
   to you." (77 chars)
   Clean classic pickup structure with a machine twist, but "every step
   brings me closer" is a generic compliment wearing ML clothes — the
   laugh barely comes from the premise.
3. "Are you my training data? Because I keep overfitting on you."
   (62 chars)
   CHOSEN. Setup and punch both live in the machine premise:
   overfitting is what a model does when it cannot stop attending to
   one example. Fresh (no batteries/charging/reboot), one breath, every
   word earns its place.
4. "Are you a transformer? Because when you attend to me, my weights
   shift." (74 chars)
   Clever, but "my weights shift" is less immediately funny than
   "overfitting on you", and "attend" does double duty awkwardly.

## Revisions to the chosen line

- v1: "Are you my training data? Because I keep overfitting on you."
  Question form; "keep overfitting" is a bit flat.
- v2: "You must be my training data, because I can't stop overfitting
  on you." — the classic "You must be X, because..." pickup frame
  reads more like a real pickup line, and "can't stop" insists harder
  than "keep".
- v3 (final): "You must be my training data, because I can't stop
  overfitting on you — and I never want to generalize." — a second
  beat from the same premise: overfitting vs. generalisation is the
  classic ML trade-off, and "I never want to generalize" turns it
  romantic: I only want this one example. Both punches come from the
  machine premise, and the line still reads in one breath.
